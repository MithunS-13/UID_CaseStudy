Exotic Charting Styles

Heikin-Ashi, Renko, and Point & Figure — advanced chart types designed to filter out market noise and reveal the true trend.

Chart Type 1

Heikin-Ashi

Heikin-Ashi (Japanese: "average bar") modifies the standard candlestick by averaging the OHLC values. This smooths out price action and makes trends much clearer.

Formula:
HA Close = (Open + High + Low + Close) / 4
HA Open = (Previous HA Open + Previous HA Close) / 2
HA High = Max(High, HA Open, HA Close)
HA Low = Min(Low, HA Open, HA Close)
Uptrend Signal

Consecutive green candles with NO lower wicks = strong uptrend. Ride the trend.

Downtrend Signal

Consecutive red candles with NO upper wicks = strong downtrend. Stay short or flat.

Reversal Signal

Small body with wicks on BOTH sides = indecision. Possible trend reversal ahead.

Chart Type 2

Renko Charts

Renko charts completely ignore TIME. A new brick is only drawn when price moves by a pre-set amount (the "brick size"). This eliminates all time-based noise.

Key Insight: During a sideways market, a Renko chart draws NOTHING. This is its superpower — you only see meaningful price moves.
Renko Advantages
  • Eliminates noise entirely
  • Trend direction crystal clear
  • Support/resistance stands out
Renko Limitations
  • No time reference on X-axis
  • Brick size is subjective
  • Misses some exact entry points
Chart Type 3

Point & Figure

Point & Figure (P&F) charts date back to the 1800s. They use X's (rising prices) and O's (falling prices) in columns. Like Renko, time is completely ignored.

How to Read P&F:
A new X is added when price rises by the "box size." A new O is added when price falls by the box size. A reversal to a new column requires price to move (box size × reversal amount) in the opposite direction.

Famous P&F Patterns

✕ A column of X's taller than the previous = bullish breakout
○ A column of O's lower than the previous = bearish breakdown
⟺ Alternating columns of similar height = sideways consolidation
