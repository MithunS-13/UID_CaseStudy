Top 5 Beginner Strategies

Visual walkthroughs of the simplest, most reliable setups to help you build a positive track record.

Before You Begin: These strategies are starting points — not guarantees. Every market is different. The goal is to give you a testable framework, not a "holy grail." Always paper trade first.
1
Most Popular

Moving Average Crossover

MA Crossover — Golden & Death Crosses

When a fast MA crosses above a slow MA (golden cross), buy. When it crosses below (death cross), sell or exit.

BUY Signal

20-day SMA crosses ABOVE 50-day SMA. Enter at close of crossover candle.

SELL Signal

20-day SMA crosses BELOW 50-day SMA. Exit the position.

Stop-Loss

Below the most recent swing low or below the 50-day SMA.

Best Markets: Trending markets. Avoid during sideways/choppy periods — generates many false signals.
2
Classic Setup

Support/Resistance Breakout

Price consolidates near a well-defined resistance level, then breaks out with strong volume. Enter on the breakout, target the height of the consolidation range projected upward.

Entry: Buy when price closes above resistance with above-average volume.
Stop: Below the breakout level.
Target: Breakout price + height of consolidation range.
False Breakout Warning: If price breaks out then immediately reverses back below resistance, exit immediately.
3
Mean Reversion

RSI Oversold Bounce

In an established uptrend, wait for the RSI to dip below 30 (oversold). When RSI crosses back above 30, buy. This is a pullback entry within a larger uptrend.

Key Rule: Only trade RSI oversold bounces in the DIRECTION of the trend. Don't use RSI to buy dips in a downtrend — you'll catch falling knives.
Entry: RSI crosses back above 30 in an uptrend
Stop: Below the most recent swing low
Target: Previous swing high or 2:1 R:R minimum
4
Trend Following

ATR-Based Trailing Stop

Use the Average True Range (ATR) to set a trailing stop that automatically adjusts based on current volatility. Higher volatility = wider stop. Lower volatility = tighter stop.

Formula: Trailing Stop = Current Price − (ATR × Multiplier)
Common multiplier: 2× or 3× ATR

Stays in winning trades longer by not getting stopped out by normal volatility, while still protecting profits when the trend reverses.

How to apply:

1
Calculate 14-period ATR for your asset
2
Enter trade in direction of the trend
3
Set stop = entry − (ATR × 2)
4
Trail the stop upward as price rises — never lower it
5
Volatility Play

Inside Day / NR4 Breakout

An Inside Day is when today's high and low are both within yesterday's range. The market is "coiling" — a breakout is coming. NR4 = Narrowest Range in 4 days (even tighter coil).

Setup Rules:
• Find a stock with an inside day (or NR4)
• Set a buy stop above today's high and sell stop below today's low
• Whichever triggers first = your trade direction
• Stop goes at the opposite extreme of the inside day
Why it works: After a period of low volatility, volatility tends to expand. The inside day is a volatility compression — the subsequent move can be significant.
Best Setup: Inside day at a key support/resistance level after a strong directional move. The combination of volatility compression AND key level makes this even more powerful.
Next Step: Pick ONE strategy from above and paper trade it exclusively for 30 days. Don't jump between strategies. Discipline and consistency are more important than finding the "perfect" setup.
