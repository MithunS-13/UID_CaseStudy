Backtesting vs. Paper Trading

The critical difference between testing historical data and forward-testing in real-time — and why you need BOTH.

⏪ Backtesting (Historical)

Applying trading rules to historical price data to see how the strategy would have performed in the past.

✓ Tests years of data instantly
✓ Reveals strategy statistics objectively
✓ Can optimize parameters across datasets
✗ Prone to "curve fitting" to past data
✗ Past performance ≠ future performance
✗ Doesn't test emotional execution

▶️ Paper Trading (Forward)

Applying your strategy to live, real-time conditions using virtual money. Decisions as if real money is at stake, but with zero financial risk.

✓ Tests in real, unfolding market conditions
✓ Includes execution psychology and timing
✓ Builds real-time decision-making skills
✗ Slow — takes months to gather data
✗ Missing emotional stakes of real money

The #1 Danger: Curve Fitting

Curve fitting (overfitting) occurs when you optimize strategy parameters to work perfectly on historical data — but the strategy fails live because it was tuned to the past, not the future.

Warning Signs:
✗ Strategy has 10+ parameters to optimize
✗ Works perfectly on backtest but fails immediately live
✗ Only tested on one asset or one time period
How to Avoid It:
✓ Keep the strategy SIMPLE (fewer rules = more robust)
✓ Test on out-of-sample data you didn't optimize on
✓ Test across multiple assets and market regimes
Golden Rule: If a strategy only works in one specific period on one specific asset, it's not a strategy — it's a coincidence.

The Correct Workflow

1
Develop a simple, logical market concept — "Breakouts from 20-day highs tend to continue."
2
Backtest without heavy optimization — Does it show positive expectancy across many stocks and time periods?
3
Out-of-sample validation — Test on data you didn't use to build the strategy. Still works? You have a real edge.
4
Paper trade for 30–60 days — Apply in real-time with virtual money. Measure execution quality.
5
Small live account — Only after consistent paper trading results, risk a small amount of real capital.
