The 7-Step Trading Process

Every successful paper trade follows this exact sequence

1
Market Analysis & Setup Identification
Analyze the chart. Identify the trend (up/down/sideways). Find a technical setup — a pattern, breakout, or indicator signal that gives you a reason to trade. This is your "thesis."
2
Define Your Risk (Stop-Loss Level)
Before thinking about profits, decide where you're WRONG. Set your stop-loss at a logical technical level (below support, above resistance). This is your maximum loss per share.
3
Calculate Position Size
Use the 1% rule: Max risk = 1% of account = $1,000 (on a $100K account). If stop is $5 away, max shares = $1,000 ÷ $5 = 200 shares. This is your position size.
4
Define Your Target (Take-Profit Level)
Set a realistic profit target. Aim for at least 2:1 reward-to-risk. If you're risking $5, your target should be at least $10 away from entry. Place it at the next key resistance level.
5
Place the Order (Entry + Bracket)
Enter the trade. Simultaneously place your stop-loss and take-profit orders. This creates a "bracket" — you're fully protected as soon as the trade is live. You can now walk away.
6
Monitor & Manage the Trade
Watch for changes in market conditions. If the trade moves in your favor, consider "trailing" your stop-loss to lock in profits. Don't move your stop-loss to AVOID a loss — this is emotional trading.
7
Close & Review
The trade exits (via TP, SL, or manual close). Review: Did price act as expected? Did you follow your plan? Write this in your trading journal. The journal is where improvement happens.
