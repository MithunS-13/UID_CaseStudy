Geometric Chart Patterns

A visual guide to Triangles, Wedges, and Head-and-Shoulders — the recurring formations that signal major price moves.

Triangle Patterns

Triangles form when price makes lower highs AND higher lows — a compression of volatility that precedes a breakout

Symmetrical Triangle

Both lines converge at equal angles. Neutral — can break either direction. Wait for the breakout and trade in that direction. Volume typically dries up as it forms.

Target: Height of the first wave, projected from the breakout point.

Ascending Triangle

Flat top (resistance) + rising lows (demand). Buyers are getting progressively more eager. Bullish bias — usually breaks upward. Very reliable pattern.

Signal: Breakout above flat resistance with expanding volume = strong buy signal.

Descending Triangle

Flat bottom (support) + falling highs (supply). Sellers are getting progressively more aggressive. Bearish bias — usually breaks downward.

Signal: Breakdown below flat support with expanding volume = strong sell signal.

Wedge Patterns

Unlike triangles, both lines in a wedge move in the SAME direction — creating a sloping range that signals exhaustion

BEARISH

Rising Wedge

Price rises but the highs and lows both converge upward. Despite the rising price, momentum is weakening. This is a "tired rally." Usually breaks DOWN.

Entry: Short when price breaks below the lower trendline of the wedge. Stop above the last high.
BULLISH

Falling Wedge

Price declines but both highs and lows converge downward. The selling is losing steam — sellers are weakening. Usually breaks UP.

Entry: Buy when price breaks above the upper trendline. Stop below the last low.

Head & Shoulders Pattern

One of the most reliable reversal patterns in all of technical analysis

Head & Shoulders (Bearish Reversal)
Left Shoulder

Price rallies to a new high, then pulls back to a support level (the "neckline"). The first peak.

The Head

Price rallies ABOVE the left shoulder to a higher new high, then falls back to the neckline again. The highest peak.

Right Shoulder

Price rallies again but FAILS to reach the head height. Falls back to the neckline. The pattern is now complete.

Neckline Break — The Signal

When price breaks below the neckline on high volume, the pattern is confirmed. Short entry!

Measured Move Target: Project the height of the head (from neckline to top) downward from the breakout point. This is your minimum target.

Inverse Head & Shoulders (Bullish Reversal)

The exact mirror image — three lows, with the middle low (head) deeper than the shoulders. When price breaks ABOVE the neckline, it's a powerful bullish reversal signal. Often appears at market bottoms.

Flags & Pennants

Continuation patterns — brief pauses in a strong trend before the move resumes

Bull Flag

A sharp rally (the "pole") followed by a sideways-to-downward consolidation (the "flag"). This is a brief rest before the rally continues. Volume drops during the flag and surges on the breakout.

Strategy: Buy the breakout above the flag's upper boundary. Target = the length of the pole.

Pennant

Like a flag, but the consolidation forms a symmetrical triangle (pennant shape) rather than a parallel channel. Very tight pattern — the breakout is usually explosive.

Key: The tighter and shorter the pennant, the more powerful the continuation move tends to be.
