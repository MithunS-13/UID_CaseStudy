Market Depth & Level 2

Visualizing the "Order Book" — the real-time list of buy and sell orders that determines where price will go next.

The Order Book (Level 2)

The order book shows all pending buy (bid) and sell (ask) orders at every price level. This is the real "supply and demand" visible in real-time before a trade executes.

Level 1 = Best bid + Best ask (what most retail traders see)
Level 2 = ALL pending orders at ALL price levels (the full order book)
Bid Side (Buyers)

Orders to BUY below the current market price. Buyers are willing to pay up to their bid price. Large bids create strong support levels.

Ask Side (Sellers)

Orders to SELL above the current market price. Sellers won't accept less than their ask price. Large asks create strong resistance levels.

The Bid-Ask Spread

The gap between the best bid and best ask. This is the transaction cost for a market order. Tight spreads = liquid market.

How to Read the Order Book

The order book reveals hidden supply and demand that standard price charts don't show

🐳

Identifying "Whales"

A single order that's 10× larger than surrounding orders is called a "wall." A large bid wall at $185 means a big buyer is there. This acts as strong support — price often stops there.

💨

Order Absorption

When a large sell order "disappears" without price moving up (it got absorbed by buyers), it's bullish. Buyers soaked up all the supply. Watch for the next move up.

🎭

Order Spoofing (Warning)

Large orders can be placed and cancelled before execution — a manipulative tactic. Large orders that appear and disappear quickly without execution should be treated with skepticism.

📊

Bid-Ask Imbalance

If there's 10× more volume on the bid side vs the ask side, buyers are dominant — bullish signal. The reverse (heavy asks) means sellers are in control.

⚡

Market Momentum (Tape)

The "Time & Sales" tape shows every executed trade in real-time. Large trades at the ask price = aggressive buyers. Large trades at the bid = aggressive sellers.

🌊

Liquidity Gaps

Price levels with very few orders in the book are "liquidity gaps." When price reaches these zones, it tends to move through them quickly — like a hot knife through butter.
