Correlation Matrix

Visualizing how different assets move together or apart — the foundation of diversification and macro trading strategy.

What is Correlation?

Correlation measures how consistently two assets move in the same direction. It's expressed as a value between -1 and +1.

+1.0 = Perfect Positive Correlation +1.0

When one asset goes up 1%, the other ALWAYS goes up 1%. They move in perfect lockstep. Example: An ETF and its underlying index.

0.0 = No Correlation 0.0

The two assets move completely independently of each other. No statistical relationship. Adding uncorrelated assets reduces portfolio risk.

-1.0 = Perfect Negative Correlation -1.0

When one asset rises, the other ALWAYS falls by the same amount. Perfect hedge. Example: VIX (fear index) vs S&P 500.

Why it matters for traders: Holding highly correlated assets gives you the ILLUSION of diversification. If AAPL and MSFT both drop when tech sells off, owning both doesn't protect you.

Famous Market Correlations

Gold vs US Dollar Index

-0.72

Gold is priced in US dollars. When the dollar strengthens, gold becomes more expensive for foreign buyers → demand falls → price falls. This inverse relationship is one of the most reliable in markets.

S&P 500 vs VIX (Fear Index)

-0.88

The VIX measures market fear. When stocks sell off (S&P falls), fear spikes (VIX rises). This is one of the strongest negative correlations in all of finance. Traders use VIX spikes to identify panic bottoms.

BTC vs ETH

+0.87

Bitcoin and Ethereum move together most of the time. Owning both doesn't provide much diversification. During crypto market downturns, both fall simultaneously. Correlation increases during crises.

Oil vs Airlines (XLE vs JETS)

-0.65

Jet fuel is one of airlines' biggest costs. When oil prices rise, airline profit margins shrink. This creates a reliably negative correlation — and a potential pair-trade opportunity for advanced traders.
