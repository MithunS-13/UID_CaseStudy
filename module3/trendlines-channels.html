Mastering Trendlines & Channels

How to draw, validate, and trade support and resistance trendlines — the most fundamental skill in technical analysis.

Interactive Trendline Chart

Rules for Drawing Valid Trendlines

A trendline is only as good as the rules used to draw it. More touches = stronger line.

Rule 1: Minimum 2 Touch Points

A trendline requires at least 2 swing highs (for resistance) or 2 swing lows (for support). The third touch CONFIRMS the line — and is often the best trade entry.

Rule 2: Don't Force the Angle

A trendline must connect actual price pivots. Never draw a line through candle bodies just to make it look "right." The slope is dictated by the market, not by you.

Rule 3: More Touches = More Valid

Each time price touches the trendline and bounces, the line gains credibility. A 5-touch trendline is much more powerful than a 2-touch line.

Rule 4: Breaks Are Significant

When price breaks through a well-established trendline with conviction (not just a wick), it's a significant signal. The old support often becomes new resistance.

Support & Resistance Explained

Support: A price level where buying demand is strong enough to prevent further decline. Price "bounces" off this floor.

Resistance: A price level where selling pressure is strong enough to halt a rally. Price "reverses" from this ceiling.

Support Becomes Resistance

When price breaks through a support level, that level often flips to become resistance. This "role reversal" is one of the most reliable phenomena in technical analysis.

Resistance Becomes Support

Conversely, when price breaks ABOVE resistance, that level often becomes new support as traders who "missed the breakout" wait to buy the pullback.

Price Channels

A channel is formed by drawing a parallel trendline on the opposite side of the price action

Ascending Channel

Higher highs and higher lows contained between two parallel rising trendlines. Buy the lower line, sell/exit at the upper line. Bullish bias.

Descending Channel

Lower highs and lower lows between two parallel declining trendlines. Bearish bias. Short at the upper line, cover at the lower line.

Horizontal Channel

Price oscillates between a flat support and resistance level. Buy support, sell resistance. Trade within the range until a breakout occurs.

Pro Tip — The Breakout Trade: The most powerful signal from a channel is a breakout. When price exits the channel with strong volume, it often travels a distance equal to the width of the channel. This is called the "measured move" target.
