Understanding Drawdowns

A drawdown is the decline from a peak in your account equity to a subsequent trough — before a new peak is established. It measures the pain of losing phases.

The Math of Recovery:
A 25% loss requires a 33% gain to recover.
A 50% loss requires a 100% gain to recover.
A 75% loss requires a 300% gain to recover.

This asymmetry is why protecting capital is the #1 priority.
Peak-to-Trough Decline

The maximum percentage drop from the highest point to the lowest subsequent point before a new high is reached. This is "maximum drawdown."

Drawdown Duration

How long the account was below its previous peak. A 3% drawdown lasting 6 months is psychologically harder than a 10% drawdown lasting 2 weeks.

Recovery Time

The time from the trough back to a new equity peak. Shorter recovery times indicate a more robust strategy.

Rules for Managing Drawdowns

📏
Pre-Define Your Max Drawdown Limit

Before you start trading, decide: "If I lose X%, I will stop and review." Common levels are 10%, 15%, or 20%. This forces objectivity when emotions run high.

📉
Reduce Size During Drawdowns

When in a drawdown, cut your position sizes in half. This slows further losses and buys time to diagnose the problem without destroying the account.

🔍
Analyze the Cause

Every drawdown has a cause: market regime change, overtrading, revenge trading, poor risk management. Identify it before returning to full size.
