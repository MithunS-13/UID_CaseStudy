Understanding R-Multiples

An "R-multiple" expresses every trade as a ratio of how much you made (or lost) vs your initial risk. It normalizes all trades to the same scale regardless of position size.

R-Multiple Formula:
If you risk $200 and make $400 → that's a +2R trade
If you risk $200 and lose $200 → that's a -1R trade
If you risk $200 and lose $400 → that's a -2R trade (stop moved!)
Target: Above +1R on wins

You should always aim for wins that are at least equal to your risk. A +2R or +3R system is highly profitable even with a 40% win rate.

Warning: Losses below -1R

If your losses are often larger than -1R, it means you're not respecting your stop-losses. This is the #1 portfolio killer for beginners.

The Kelly Criterion — Optimal Position Sizing

The Kelly Criterion is a mathematical formula that calculates the optimal fraction of your capital to risk per trade to maximize long-term growth.

Kelly % = W − [(1 − W) / R]

Where: W = Win Rate, R = Win/Loss Ratio

Example: 58% Win Rate, 2.3:1 R:R
Kelly = 0.58 − (0.42 / 2.3) = 0.58 − 0.18 = 40%
Practical Advice: Never bet the full Kelly amount. Use "Half Kelly" (20% here) to reduce volatility. Full Kelly leads to massive drawdowns that are psychologically unbearable.
