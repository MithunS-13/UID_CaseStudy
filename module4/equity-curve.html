Reading Your Equity Curve

The equity curve is the visual autobiography of your trading system. Its shape tells you everything about your strategy's health.

Ideal: Smooth, Rising Curve

A gently sloping upward curve with small pullbacks indicates a consistent, robust strategy. This is what every trader strives for.

Warning: Volatile, Jagged Curve

Large swings up and down indicate inconsistency. Even if profitable overall, this pattern suggests poor risk management and will be psychologically devastating.

Flat Periods = Drawdown Phases

Extended flat or declining periods on the curve are drawdowns. Every strategy has them. The key is their depth and duration.

Always Compare to a Benchmark

If your equity curve barely beats the S&P 500, why take the risk of active trading? Your goal is meaningful alpha — outperformance above the benchmark.

Key Performance Metrics

The numbers that professional traders and fund managers use to evaluate any strategy

📊 Sharpe Ratio

Measures return per unit of risk (volatility). Higher is better. A Sharpe above 1.0 is good; above 2.0 is excellent.

Sharpe = (Return − Risk-Free Rate) / Std Dev
Your Sharpe: 1.42 (Good)

📉 Max Drawdown

The largest peak-to-trough decline in your equity curve. This tells you the worst-case scenario for your strategy from any starting point.

Max DD: -12.3% ($12,300)

📈 Calmar Ratio

Annual return divided by maximum drawdown. Measures return per unit of drawdown risk. Above 1.0 is considered reasonable.

Calmar = Annual Return / Max Drawdown
Your Calmar: 46.8% / 12.3% = 3.8

🎯 Win Rate

Percentage of trades that are profitable. A 50% win rate with 2:1 reward-to-risk is highly profitable. Win rate alone means nothing without knowing the R:R ratio.

💹 Profit Factor

Total gross profit divided by total gross losses. Must be above 1.0 to be profitable. Above 1.5 is good; above 2.0 is excellent.

Profit Factor = Gross Wins / Gross Losses
Your PF: $28,400 / $14,200 = 2.0

⏱️ Recovery Factor

Net profit divided by maximum drawdown. Tells you how efficiently your strategy recovers from losses. Higher = more resilient strategy.

Recovery = Net Profit / Max Drawdown
Your RF: $23,400 / $12,300 = 1.9
